## How the master data seeder works

1. **Generate.** Countries, currencies, categories, stores and warehouses come from `reference_data/`. Products, their opening prices and customers come from the seed in `config/generator.toml`. Product n and customer n each draw from their own random stream, so the pilot (20 products, 100 customers) is exactly the start of the full dataset (500, 20,000).
2. **Validate.** Every value is checked against the schema rules of V003 to V005 and the prerequisites of V006 and V007, plus data quality rules the schema cannot express. Any issue stops the run.
3. **Preview.** CSV files and `summary.json` (with a SHA-256 fingerprint of the whole dataset) go to `output/master_data/<profile>/`.
4. **Plan** (`--plan`, read-only). Each row is matched on its business key: present and equal, missing, or present with other values (conflict).
5. **Apply** (`--apply`). In one transaction under an advisory lock: plan again, insert the missing rows in foreign key order, re-read and compare every row, COMMIT. Nothing is ever updated or deleted. A conflict or any error writes nothing.

## How the opening inventory generator works

1. **Generate.** Every product is listed at all 12 stores and 4 warehouses. Expected weekly demand comes from the category demand and reference price in `config/generator.toml` (`[inventory]`), the product price and a seeded popularity, split by the customer mix per market, the online share (warehouses) and the store format. Stock is a number of weeks of that demand with seeded variation, never below the display units of a store or the minimum of a warehouse. Products launched after the opening day are listed at 0.
2. **Validate.** Completeness, references, V007 rules, launch and opening dates, reorder points, display minimums, warehouse cover and capacity. Any issue stops the run.
3. **Preview.** `output/opening_inventory/<profile>/opening_inventory.csv` and `summary.json` with a fingerprint.
4. **Plan** (`--plan`, read-only). Requires the master data to be present and unchanged. Each listing is matched on location and product; its `opening_balance` movement is compared.
5. **Apply** (`--apply`). In one transaction under an advisory lock: plan again, insert the missing `inventory_balances` rows at 0, insert the `opening_balance` movements in the V007 lock order (the V007 trigger adds them to stock on hand), re-read and compare every listing, COMMIT. Quantities are never written directly. A conflict or any error writes nothing.

## How the pilot transaction simulation works

1. **Generate.** The scenario mix in `[transactions.scenarios]` (20 orders) is spread over the order window. Each order gets a channel, a market, a time (online hours, or store opening hours and never on a Sunday), a fulfilment location (the market warehouse online, the store's own stock in store) and an active customer registered before the order. Lines are chosen at placing time from the products on sale with free stock at that location, weighted by its expected demand. Prices, costs and VAT come from the master data valid at placed_at; totals and the shipping fee follow the V006 formulas. Payments and every timestamp follow the scenario. A simulated stock ledger, replayed in business-time order, decides availability, so every U2 result is known in advance.
2. **Validate.** References, customer, product, location and date consistency, money, payment rules, lifecycle timing, and an independent replay of the stock. Any issue stops the run.
3. **Preview.** `output/transactions/<profile>/` with orders, lines, payment attempts, the units of work in run order, and `summary.json` with a fingerprint.
4. **Plan** (`--plan`, read-only). Requires the master data and the opening inventory to be present and unchanged. For each order, works out from the database which steps are done, compares every value, and checks that the done steps are a prefix of the plan and that the stock equals the opening stock replayed through them.
5. **Apply** (`--apply`). Under a session advisory lock, runs every remaining unit of work as its own transaction, in business-time order. Each step uses V006/V007 functions and guards: `fn_reserve_order_stock`, `fn_close_order_reservations` and `fn_fulfil_order_stock`, plus guarded status updates. Each result is compared with the plan. A different result rolls that step back and stops; the next run continues there. At the end every order, payment, reservation, sale and stock balance is verified.

After the pilot, the transaction loader accepts purchase receipts and transfers dated after the plan's last event (later replenishment) and adds them to its expected stock, so `--plan` keeps verifying the pilot. It still refuses them while pilot steps are missing (business-time order) and refuses any receipt or transfer dated inside the pilot.

## How the replenishment pilot works

Design and decisions: `docs/phase3_replenishment_feasibility.md` (D1 to D7, D9 to D11 adopted; D8 deferred).

1. **Generate.** Start from the stock after the pilot (opening inventory replayed through the 100 pilot units of work). R0 revises every warehouse reorder point to echelon demand (online plus the market's stores, lead time x safety factor, below the opening quantity). Stores are reviewed every evening (Monday to Saturday): a listing whose inventory position (on hand - reserved + requested + in transit) is at or below its reorder point asks for S - IP units. The market warehouse dispatches the next working morning (stores at 0 first, then flagships; never below its own reorder point) and the transfer arrives before the store opens. Warehouses are reviewed on Mondays: natural purchase orders when IP <= reorder point, plus the pilot's forced purchase orders (one per market, flagged `forced_reorder`); deliveries arrive 10 to 14 days later, the SE one split into two notes. Products not launched yet are never replenished outside their pre-launch window.
2. **Validate.** Independent checks: pilot base, window and business-time order, echelon reorder points, document formats and numbering, routes, calendar and launch rules, triggers (natural vs forced), case packs, split delivery, and a stock replay with conservation after every unit of work.
3. **Preview.** `output/replenishment/<profile>/`: reorder points, transfers, purchase orders, receipts, units of work, reviews, `summary.json` with a fingerprint.
4. **Plan** (`--plan`, read-only). Requires master data, opening inventory and the complete pilot transactions. Matches every planned movement on its business key (type, location, product, document reference; arrivals through their transfer_out), compares values, requires a prefix in business-time order and the stock to equal the after-pilot stock replayed through the done units of work.
5. **Apply** (`--apply`). Under an advisory lock, each remaining unit of work in its own transaction: R0 guarded reorder point update, R1 receipts, T1 dispatches, T2 arrivals, with balance rows locked in (location, product) order. Each result is compared with the plan; a difference rolls back and stops; the next run continues. At the end every movement, balance, reorder point and the conservation identity are verified.

## Rules from the approved design that the code follows

- Money uses `decimal.Decimal` with `ROUND_HALF_UP`, never `float` or banker's rounding, so values match the CHECK constraints and the Power BI rounding.
- Reproducible randomness: text-seeded `random.Random` streams per entity, stable methods only, Decimal instead of float `exp`/`log`/`pow`. The same seed gives the same fingerprint on Python 3.10 to 3.14.
- Every unit of work is one transaction; retry only on SQLSTATE 40001, 40P01 and 55P03; never retry 23514 or 23503; treat 23505 on an idempotency index as "already done" and reload state.
- Provider calls (payments, refunds) are never made while holding row locks.
- Inventory steps (from V007), for the next phase steps:
  - reserve with `fn_reserve_order_stock` (U2). On `out_of_stock`, roll back, then cancel the order with reason `out_of_stock` in a new transaction;
  - release or expire with `fn_close_order_reservations` (U3, U4);
  - fulfil with `fn_fulfil_order_stock` (U5).

  Each is idempotent. Every `purchase_receipt`, `stock_adjustment`, `write_off` and `transfer_out` needs a document number in `external_reference`. Each location and product gets one `opening_balance`, written by the opening inventory generator.
- The activity generator must write in date order and never write `inventory_balances` quantities; they follow from the movements and reservations.
- Receipts and transfers (U7) are inserts only: `purchase_receipt` with `external_reference` = `<PO number>/DN<n>`, `transfer_out` and `transfer_in` with `external_reference` = `TR-<market>-<year>-<seq>-<destination>`; the transfer_in points to its transfer_out (`related_movement_id`). A 23505 on `uq_inventory_movements_document_line` or `uq_inventory_movements_one_transfer_in_per_out` means the step already happened.
